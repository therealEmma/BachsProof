---
title: "Database transactions and financial integrity"
description: "Make payment checks and ledger writes succeed or fail together."
---

A database transaction groups related writes into one unit. For payment
processing, the usual unit is: confirm that this event is eligible, update the
payment state, and create the ledger entry. The application should not commit
only half of that work.

## A safe transaction shape

```text
begin transaction
  lock or uniquely claim the payment
  re-check its status and amount
  if already credited: do nothing
  otherwise write the credit and mark the payment completed
commit
```

The exact SQL, ORM, and lock depend on your application. BachsProof does not
choose them for you. It sends duplicates, wrong values, ineligible events, and
concurrent requests, then reads the committed result through `verify()`.

## Constraints are part of the design

Use a database uniqueness rule where one is needed, such as a unique key on an
eligible payment's credit identity. A code-only check can still fail when two
workers run together. A constraint turns the second attempt into a safe
duplicate path that your handler can handle deliberately.

## Why this matters to BachsProof

`INV-001` checks that one payment is not credited more than once. `INV-003`
checks that an eligible payment eventually receives its credit. `INV-004` and
`INV-005` check that rejection and totals are correct. Those rules are only as
strong as the committed records returned by your verifier.
