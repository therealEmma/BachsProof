---
title: "Financial ledgers"
description: "Use durable financial entries to make payment results explainable."
---

A ledger is the durable record of money that your application granted, reserved,
refunded, or moved. It can be a `walletCredit` table, an accounting journal, or
another design. BachsProof does not require a particular schema. It requires
that `verify()` can read enough committed evidence to answer: what happened to
this payment?

## Why a ledger matters

An HTTP `200` only says that your route answered. It does not prove that:

- the payment was found;
- the amount and currency were correct;
- the payment was credited once;
- the credit was committed before the response; or
- a failed or expired payment was rejected.

A ledger makes those answers inspectable. For an eligible NGN 5,000 payment,
the verifier should be able to see one credit for `500000` minor units in NGN.

## What BachsProof reads

Your `verify(reference)` callback can return a small summary plus ledger rows:

```js
{
  creditCount: 1,
  creditedAmountMinor: 500000,
  currency: "NGN",
  status: "COMPLETED",
  pending: false,
  ledgerEntries: [{ id: "credit-1", type: "CREDIT", amountMinor: 500000, currency: "NGN" }]
}
```

BachsProof compares this observed state with the scenario's expected result.
It does not infer an amount or currency from the configuration when your
records are missing. See [Verifying financial records](/testing/verifying-financial-records)
for a complete database example.

## Keep the evidence tied to the payment

Every ledger entry should be traceable to one application payment reference.
Do not calculate a balance for the whole test wallet and assume it belongs to
the event under test. Fresh references and payment-scoped queries let
BachsProof distinguish one payment from another.
