---
title: "INV-002: Amount Integrity"
description: "A posted credit must match the expected amount and currency."
---

## What it means

If the test expects an NGN ₦5,000 payment, the observed aggregate should be
`500_000` minor units and currency `NGN`. NGN ₦4,999, ₦5,001, or a different
currency is not the same payment result.

```text
Passing: expected 500000 NGN; observed 500000 NGN; creditCount 1
Failing: expected 500000 NGN; observed 499900 NGN; creditCount 1
```

That is one ₦5,000 credit passing and one ₦4,999 credit failing. An observed
`500_000 USD` also fails because currency is part of the financial value.

## How BachsProof evaluates it

When at least one credit exists, BachsProof compares
`observed.creditedAmountMinor` and `observed.currency` with the expected state.
Values are integers so a floating-point rounding error cannot hide a mismatch.
The rule checks the normalized aggregate returned by the callback; it does not
sum arbitrary database rows itself.

Investigate order amount matching, currency checks, decimal-to-minor
conversion, and whether fees were incorrectly included in the customer credit.

This rule is most useful with `incorrect-payment-amount`,
`incorrect-payment-currency`, and the duplicate scenarios. A zero-credit
ineligible event does not enter this comparison; it is covered by `INV-004`.
